# The Adventure of the Speckled Band — Fact Extraction

Source: `source.txt`. The file has no paragraph numbers, so citations use line numbers (`L###`) from that file. Story set early April 1883 (L53). Narrator is Dr. Watson.

## 1. Characters

**Sherlock Holmes** — consulting detective, Baker Street. Late riser (L54). Keeps a case-book (L139). Strong enough to straighten a bent steel poker (L560). Knows of Palmer and Pritchard, doctor-poisoners (L957).

**Dr. John Watson** — narrator, Holmes's flatmate. Carries a revolver (Eley's No. 2) at Holmes's request (L588). Notices the bell-rope and ventilator but draws no conclusion from them (L912–919).

**Helen Stoner** — client, about 30 by appearance (L101); "hair shot with premature grey" (L101).
- Stepdaughter of Roylott and twin of Julia.
- Engaged to Percy Armitage, second son of Mr. Armitage of Crane Water, near Reading (L399–401). Wedding expected "in the course of the spring" (L402).
- Heard of Holmes through Mrs. Farintosh (L130).
- Called "Miss Roylott" once by Holmes (L420), otherwise "Miss Stoner".
- Secret: hides the bruises Roylott gave her ("five little livid spots" on the wrist, L425). Holmes says she is "screening your stepfather" (L420).

**Dr. Grimesby Roylott** — stepfather and culprit.
- Last of an old Saxon family, the Roylotts of Stoke Moran. The family fortune was lost; the house is mortgaged (L170–176).
- Took a medical degree on an advance from a relative and practised in Calcutta (L178–181).
- Beat his native butler to death in a rage over robberies and served a long prison term (L181–185).
- Violent and "absolutely uncontrollable in his anger" (L213). Huge and strong. Bent the poker (L552–553).
- Keeps a cheetah and a baboon, sent from India by a correspondent (L222–224), and keeps friendly with wandering gipsies (L218–221).
- Smokes strong Indian cigars (L267).
- Appearance: very tall and broad, face "marked with every evil passion", a nose like "a fierce old bird of prey" (L509–513).
- Motive: money. His income depends on his late wife's estate, which reduces if either stepdaughter marries (see §3).
- Secret: keeps a swamp adder, trained to return to a whistle, and uses it to kill. Killed Julia and tried to kill Helen.

**Julia Stoner** — Helen's twin, dead two years before the story, aged 30 (L229).
- Engaged to a half-pay major of marines she met at Christmas at Harrow (L240–242).
- Died within a fortnight of the wedding day (L243–244).
- Last words: "Oh, my God! Helen! It was the band! The speckled band!" (L331).

**Mrs. Stoner (later Mrs. Roylott)** — the twins' mother, young widow of Major-General Stoner, Bengal Artillery (L189). Married Roylott in India. Income not less than £1000 a year (L192). Killed eight years ago in a railway accident near Crewe (L196).

**Miss Honoria Westphail** — the twins' maternal aunt, lives near Harrow (L238–239). Helen is taken to her after the death (L1108).

**Percy Armitage** — Helen's fiancé. Appears only by name.

**Mrs. Farintosh** — former client of Holmes (opal tiara case). Appears only by name (L131, L143).

**Major of marines** — Julia's fiancé. Unnamed.

**Housekeeper** — "old and foolish" (L444–445). Helen can easily get her out of the way. The bell-rope is said to lead to her room (L724).

**The driver** — a lad who drives Roylott's trap. Roylott shouts at him over the gates (L887–890).

**Unnamed driver at Leatherhead** — takes Holmes and Watson to Stoke Moran (L614).

**Landlord of the Crown Inn** — told Holmes and Watson are going on a "late visit" (L972–974).

**The gipsies** — encamped in the plantation on the estate, "nearly always" (L381). Roylott is their only friend (L218). They wear spotted handkerchiefs (L388–389).

**Animals**
- Cheetah: heard at the window during the vigil (L1030).
- Baboon: runs across the lawn (L982–993).
- Swamp adder: Roylott's snake. Holmes calls it "the deadliest snake in India" and says death follows within ten seconds of a bite (L1092–1093).

## 2. Locations

**Baker Street (Holmes and Watson's rooms)** — sitting-room with fireplace, Holmes's desk (L139) and a poker used in Roylott's demonstration. Mrs. Hudson is the landlady.

**Leatherhead station / station inn** — Helen reached it at 6:20 by dog-cart, then took the first train to Waterloo (L125–127). Holmes and Watson later hire a trap here.

**Doctors' Commons** — where Holmes reads the will (L567–575).

**Stoke Moran Manor House** (western border of Surrey, L165)
- Two-hundred-year-old house under heavy mortgage (L175).
- Grey, lichen-blotched stone, a high central portion and two curving wings "like the claws of a crab" (L659–661).
- One wing has broken windows, boarded up, and a partly collapsed roof. The right-hand block is "comparatively modern" and is the inhabited part (L661–665).
- Scaffolding on the end wall; masonry broken into; no workmen present (L666–668). Helen says the repairs were not needed and were "an excuse to move me from my room" (L679–682).
- Only one wing inhabited. Bedrooms are on the ground floor, sitting-rooms in the central block (L255–257).
- The three bedrooms open onto one corridor and do not connect to each other (L258–259). Their windows open onto the lawn (L264).
- Order from the main building: Roylott's room (nearest the main block), the middle room (Julia's, now Helen's), Helen's old room (end, next to the scaffolding) (L257–258, L672–676).
- The corridor runs along the other side of the wing. Its windows are very small, "too narrow for anyone to pass through" (L686–688).
- Entry to the corridor is by a small side door (L704).

**Julia's room (middle room)** (L704–718)
- Low ceiling, gaping fireplace. The chimney is "barred up by four large staples" (L365).
- Brown chest of drawers, narrow white-counterpaned bed, dressing-table to the left of the window, two wicker chairs, a square of Wilton carpet. Worm-eaten oak boards and panelling.
- Shutters are old-fashioned with broad iron bars. Holmes cannot force them from outside, no slit for a knife, hinges are solid iron set in masonry (L694–701).
- Bell-rope beside the bed, tassel lying on the pillow. It looks newer, was put up "a couple of years ago", and is a dummy not attached to a wire (L720–748). It is fastened to a hook just above the ventilator (L749–750).
- Ventilator opens into Roylott's room, not outside. It is "quite modern", from about the same time as the bell-rope (L756–763). Watson calls it so small that "a rat could hardly pass through" (L918–919).
- Bed is clamped to the floor (stated by Holmes at L944; not mentioned in the room description at L704–718).

**Roylott's room** (L770–776)
- Larger than the others, plainly furnished: camp-bed, small shelf of mostly technical books, armchair beside the bed, plain wooden chair against the wall, round table, large iron safe.
- Saucer of milk on top of the safe (L790–791).
- Small dog lash hung on a corner of the bed, curled and tied into a loop of whipcord (L804–806).
- On the night: dark-lantern on the table lit on the safe, safe door ajar (L1074–1076).

**Helen's old room (end room)** — Holmes refuses to examine it (L705). Helen is to retire there on the night of the vigil (L850–852).

**Lawn and grounds** — ill-trimmed lawn, laurel bushes, plantation where the gipsies camp, breaches in the old park wall (L979–980), iron gates on the avenue.

**The Crown Inn** (village; opposite the house per L412, "opposite") — upper-floor rooms with a view of the avenue gate and the inhabited wing (L883–886). Helen got her dog-cart here (L412). From the inn the middle window is visible.

**Harrow** — aunt's house; refuge for Helen.

## 3. True timeline

**Before the story**
1. Roylott's family ruined over several generations (L170–176).
2. Roylott trains as a doctor and practises in Calcutta (L178–181).
3. He beats his native butler to death after robberies, serves a long term in prison, and returns to England (L181–186).
4. In India he marries the widowed Mrs. Stoner. The twins are two years old (L188–191). Her income is not less than £1000 a year, left entirely to Roylott while the daughters live with him, with an annual sum due to each on marriage (L191–194).
5. On return to England, Mrs. Roylott is killed in a railway accident near Crewe, eight years before the story (L195–196).
6. Roylott fails to establish a London practice and moves the girls into the Stoke Moran house (L196–198).
7. He turns reclusive and violent: brawls, two police-court cases, quarrels with villagers (L202–213).
8. Roylott keeps Indian animals and associates with the gipsies (L218–225).
9. Roughly two years before the story, "a couple of years ago", the bell-rope, the ventilator and other "little changes" are made in the middle room (L728, L763).
10. Christmas, two years before the story: Julia meets the major of marines at Harrow and becomes engaged. Roylott raises no objection (L240–244).
11. In the "last few nights" before her death, Julia hears a low, clear whistle at about 3 a.m. (L282–284). Roylott is in his room smoking Indian cigars the night she dies (L265–268).
12. **Night of Julia's death** (within a fortnight of the wedding):
    - Julia goes to Helen's room, then at 11 p.m. returns to her own room and locks her door (L269, L295–297).
    - Wild night, wind and rain (L314–315).
    - Helen is awake. A scream; Helen opens her door and hears a low whistle, then a clang (L318–320).
    - Julia's door unlocks and opens; Julia staggers out, says "the speckled band!", points at Roylott's door, collapses and dies without regaining consciousness (L321–339).
    - Roylott comes from his room in a dressing-gown and sends for a doctor (L335–338).
    - In Julia's hands: a charred match stump (right) and a match-box (left) (L351–352).
13. The inquiry finds no cause of death. Door fastened inside, shutters barred, walls and floor sound, chimney barred, no marks of violence, no poison found (L358–372). Helen's evidence on the whistle is uncertain (L345–347).
14. Two years pass (L397).

**Story-present**
15. About a month ago, Percy Armitage proposes to Helen. Roylott does not oppose it (L398–403).
16. Two days before the visit, "repairs" begin in the west wing, piercing Helen's bedroom wall. She moves into Julia's room and sleeps in Julia's bed (L403–406).
17. Night before the visit: Helen lies awake and hears the low whistle. She lights a lamp and sees nothing (L406–410).
18. Early April 1883, before 6 a.m.: Helen leaves, reaches Leatherhead at 6:20, takes the first train to Waterloo (L125–127). She tells Holmes the story at about 7:15–7:30 (L55).
19. Roylott, who has "traced" Helen, enters Baker Street, threatens Holmes and bends the poker (L520–556).
20. Holmes visits Doctors' Commons, reads the will, and returns shortly before 1 p.m. (L571–591).
21. Holmes and Watson take the train to Leatherhead, a trap to Stoke Moran, and meet Helen outside. They examine the grounds and rooms in the afternoon (L593–815).
22. At dusk Roylott returns by trap (L886–890). About 9 p.m. his light goes out (L964).
23. At 11 p.m. Helen signals from the middle window (L966–970). Holmes and Watson enter by the window, glimpse the baboon on the way, and sit in the dark in the middle room.
24. About 3 a.m. (after the parish clock strikes twelve, one, two, three, L1033): a dark-lantern gleams at the ventilator, then a smell of burning oil and hot metal. About half an hour later a soft hissing is heard (L1036–1043).
25. Holmes strikes a match and lashes the bell-rope with his cane. Watson hears a low whistle. A terrible cry follows from Roylott's room (L1043–1061).
26. They find Roylott dead on the chair in his room, with the yellow, speckled snake coiled around his head, the dark-lantern shining on the open safe and the lash across his lap (L1074–1090).
27. Holmes loops the snake with the whipcord, throws it into the safe and shuts it (L1099–1102).
28. Next morning Helen is taken by train to Harrow. The official inquiry concludes Roylott was "indiscreetly playing with a dangerous pet" (L1106–1110).
29. Holmes's explanation to Watson on the train home next day (L1111 onward).

**Narrative frame**: Watson records the story years later, freed from a promise of secrecy by Helen's death "during the last month" (L44–48).

**Text-internal discrepancies** (recorded without comment):
- The twins were two years old at their mother's remarriage and she died eight years ago, yet Julia was 30 at her death and Helen appears about 30 now (L190, L196, L229, L101).
- The mother's income is "not less than £1000" (L192) and "little short of £1,100" (L578).

## 5. Reasoning chain

Steps are in the order Holmes states them or hints at them. ⚠ = uses knowledge the reader never received.

**S1.** Premises: C13 (the room was sealed), C14 → Julia was alone in the room (stated by Helen at L366 and Watson at L472–475).

**S2.** Premises: C7, C9, C11, C14, C15, C23 → first theory (wrong): gipsies are involved. Whistle as signal, metallic clang as a bar falling back, "band" as the gipsy band (L482–490). Holmes admits many objections (L496–500).

**S3.** Premises: C3, C20 → Roylott has a "very strongest" financial motive to stop both marriages (L583–585).

**S4.** Premises: C23, C22, C8, C13 → nothing can come through the window or door; the danger "could not come either from the window or the door" (L1121–1122), which disproves S2.

**S5.** Premises: C6 (cigar smell reached Julia's room) → there must be a small opening between the two rooms (L926–930). Confirmed by C26. Holmes states this inference at L921–930.

**S6.** Premises: C24, C25, C26, C32 → the rope is not a bell-pull; it is "a bridge for something passing through the hole and coming to the bed" (L1125–1127). The bed is clamped so the occupant lies in a fixed position relative to the rope (L949–951).

**S7.** Premises: S6, C4 (Indian animals), C2 (Roylott's Indian training), C13 (no poison found), C45 ⚠ → the "something" is a snake (L1128–1130). The inference uses knowledge never given to the reader: that a snake could climb a rope, that its venom would escape a chemical test, and that bite marks would be small and overlooked. No puncture marks were ever reported.

**S8.** Premises: C7, C9 (whistle), C28 (milk), S7 ⚠ → Roylott trained the snake with milk to return to a whistle (L1137–1141). This uses outside knowledge of snake behaviour. The milk's meaning depends on S7.

**S9.** Premises: C7 (whistle at about 3 a.m.), S8 → the whistle recalls the snake "before the morning light revealed it to the victim" (L1137–1139).

**S10.** Premises: C29 ⚠ (result of the chair inspection, undisclosed), C26 → Roylott stood on the chair to reach the ventilator (L1146–1149). The reader is not told what Holmes saw on the chair.

**S11.** Premises: C9 (clang), C27 (safe), S7 → the clang was the safe door being shut on the snake (L1151–1153). The safe's role is inferred only after S7.

**S12.** Premises: C30 (looped lash), S8 → the lash with its loop is the tool for handling the snake. Holmes mentions it only as dispelling doubts (L1149–1150) and uses it to noose the snake at L1099–1101.

**S13.** Premises: C11 (speckled band), C12 (match), S7 → Julia struck a light, saw the snake on her bed and called it "the speckled band" (L1116–1119). This is stated only in the after-the-fact explanation.

**S14.** Premises: C21 (repairs as an excuse), C17, S6–S9 → Roylott moved Helen into the death room to repeat the method (implied at L403–406 and L678–684; Holmes calls it "suggestive", L683).

**S15.** Premises: S7–S14 → the plan to test the theory: spend the night in the room, wait in the dark, strike at the rope when the hissing begins (L861–862, L1043–1045). Confirmation is by C37–C41.

**Knowledge the reader never received (⚠):**
- Result of Holmes's inspection of the wooden chair (C29).
- That the bed is clamped to the floor is told only in conversation (C32).
- No mention anywhere of puncture marks on Julia's body (C45).
- Snake behaviour (response to a whistle and milk, climbing a rope, venom undetectable by chemical test).
- What Holmes saw on the floor and panelling with the lens (L737–741): never stated.
- What Holmes sees on the rope at L1047.

## 6. Red herrings

**R1. The gipsies as culprits** — raised by Holmes at L482–490 (whistle, band, clang, presence in the plantation). Dispelled when the shutters cannot be forced (C23) and the rooms are shown unapproachable (L1121–1122). Holmes calls this "an entirely erroneous conclusion" (L1114).

**R2. "Speckled band" as the spotted gipsy handkerchief** — offered by Helen (L386–390). Dispelled when the band proves to be a snake (L1086–1090).

**R3. Julia died of fright/nervous shock** — Helen's belief (L376–377). Holmes rejects it: "some more tangible cause" (L877–878). Dispelled by the snake.

**R4. The whistle came from the lawn or from sleep-whistling** — Julia's suggestions (L277–290). Dispelled when the whistle recurs inside the room (L1049).

**R5. Helen may have misheard the whistle and clang** — raised at the inquiry and again by Holmes (L342–347). Both noises are later explained (S9, S11).

**R6. The metallic clang as a shutter-bar falling back** — Holmes's own first guess (L486–489). Explained later as the safe door (L1151–1153).

**R7. Poison by conventional means** — doctors' examination found none (L370–372). Dispelled by the snake venom explanation.

**R8. The cheetah and baboon as threats** — Helen locks doors against them (L306–307). Neither plays a part in the deaths; the baboon only startles Watson (L982–993).

## 7. Solution

**Culprit**: Dr. Grimesby Roylott. He is killed by his own snake on the night of the vigil.

**Method**
- Roylott has an Indian snake (identified as a swamp adder).
- A dummy bell-rope and a ventilator link his room to the middle bedroom. The bed is clamped to the floor beneath the rope.
- At night he stands on his chair and puts the snake through the ventilator. It crawls down the rope and onto the bed (L1140–1142).
- Roylott whistles to recall the snake before morning, using milk training (L1139–1141). A hiss and the smell of the lantern accompany the attempt.
- The venom leaves no trace detectable by tests, and the two punctures are easily missed (L1131–1137).
- After Julia's death he shut the snake in the iron safe; the clang Helen heard was the safe door (L1151–1153).

**Motive**: money. Under Mrs. Roylott's will, each daughter can claim £250 a year on marriage out of a total income of about £750, so one marriage would "cripple him" and two would leave him a "mere pittance" (L578–583). Julia's wedding was close; Helen's engagement was recent. Helen was moved into the death room two days before the story (L403–406).

**Proof/confirmation**
- Holmes and Watson sit in the dark in the middle room. The dark-lantern gleams at the ventilator, then there is a hiss (L1036–1043).
- Holmes strikes a light and lashes the rope. The snake retreats through the ventilator and bites Roylott, who shrieks (L1158–1163).
- In Roylott's room, the snake is coiled round his head as a "speckled band" (L1088–1090). The safe is open, the lash is across his lap and the lantern is lit.
- Holmes loops the snake and shuts it in the safe (L1099–1102).
- The official verdict was misadventure with a dangerous pet (L1109–1110). No formal charge is brought against Roylott for Julia's death.
- Holmes accepts "indirect" responsibility for Roylott's death (L1161–1165).
